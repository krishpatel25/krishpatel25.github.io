01 · THE WORKLOAD

Nothing about a computer matters until something runs on it.

The top layer is the job itself. Right now that job is increasingly machine learning — and ML is unusually sensitive to the hardware underneath it, which is what makes this layer worth understanding before you design anything below it.

Making a compiler search smarter

IBM-ILLINOIS DISCOVERY ACCELERATOR · 2024–2025

A tensor compiler has to choose how to lay a computation onto hardware, and the number of possible choices is enormous. I used language-model techniques to prune that search space, then rebuilt the legacy pipeline on a modern framework — and proved the rebuild agreed with the original to within two percent.

03 · THE MACHINE

This is the layer I care about most.

Underneath the operating system is the processor itself — the thing that decides, thousands of times a second, what to do next and in what order. I built one that does this out of order, guessing at the future and undoing the guess when it's wrong.

FLAGSHIP

A processor that runs ahead of itself

ECE 411 · SYSTEMVERILOG

Most processors wait. This one doesn't: when an instruction stalls, it finds later work that's ready and does that instead, then puts the results back in order before anyone notices. It predicts which way a branch will go, and when it guesses wrong it unwinds the mistake early instead of paying for it. Benchmarked on CoreMark against a hand-written test suite.
